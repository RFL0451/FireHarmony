Harmonizing MODIS and VIIRS for Global Fire Monitoring

How combining two satellite sensors gives us a fuller, more accurate picture of fires on Earth

Explore the Data

Two sensors, two ways of seeing fire

MODIS and VIIRS both detect thermal anomalies, the "hot spots" that usually mean active fires. Their data cannot simply be merged, because they differ in three ways

  • Spatial resolutionMODIS sees the ground in 1 km pixels; VIIRS in 375 m pixels
  • Operational periodMODIS has recorded fires since 2000; VIIRS since 2012
  • Detection algorithmsEach sensor has its own thresholds, confidence scales and quirks

MODIS and VIIRS side by side

PropertyMODISVIIRS
Resolution1 km375 m (I-bands)
OperationalTerra (data since 2000), Aqua (since 2002)Suomi NPP (since 2012), NOAA-20 and later
SensitivityStrong on large, intense firesBetter on small, low-intensity fires
LimitationsCoarse pixels miss small, cool firesShorter record; saturates on very intense fires

Why resolution changes what we see

Resize the fire, or click the grid to move it. A small fire can hide inside one 1 km MODIS pixel but fill a large share of a 375 m VIIRS pixel

Simplified illustration: a pixel lights up when the fire covers at least 3% of it. Real algorithms also use brightness temperature and surrounding context

Why not use only VIIRS?

MODIS is the long record

Its 20+ years of continuous observations make it the "gold standard" for long-term climate and fire trends

VIIRS sees small fires

Finer pixels catch small, low-intensity fires, but very large intense fires can saturate the sensor and limit intensity estimates

Each misses something

Every sensor misses fires the other detects. Together they close the blind spots

Each sensor has detections the other misses

Fires seen by both sensors give us confidence. Fires seen by only one are exactly what a single-sensor record would lose

Our solution: harmonization

Harmonization turns two different records into one unified dataset

  1. Align formats

    Bring both products into one common set of fields

  2. Align timestamps

    Put every detection on the same UTC time scale

  3. Match and merge

    Detections within 1 km and 3 hours are one fire. Detections only one sensor saw are kept

Project goals

  1. Build a unified fire record for historical and geographic analysis
  2. Identify periods of anomalously high fire activity by region

Useful for fire managers, climate researchers and anyone who needs a longer, fuller fire record
